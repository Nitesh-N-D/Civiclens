# CivicLens pilot test plan

**Status:** Draft plan only; no participants recruited  
**Geography:** Tamil Nadu  
**Pilot language:** Tamil first, English comparison

## Readiness gates

Do not test scheme recommendations as live guidance until each pilot record has current controlling department evidence, source access/reuse review, passage-level citation, human review, and fluent Tamil review. Before that, limit tasks to finding and understanding official directory pages and state plainly that eligibility rules are unverified.

## Research questions

1. Can a participant find an official source and understand the next step more easily with CivicLens?
2. Which parts of the current journey cause confusion: awareness, eligibility language, documents, application route, language, or trust?
3. Does Tamil-first wording help users understand the conditions, including exclusions and uncertainty?
4. Do participants understand that CivicLens is independent and cannot decide eligibility?
5. When do users prefer e-Sevai staff, college staff, a department office, or another human helper?

## Participants and tasks

Recruit 10–20 adults who voluntarily opt in, with variation across students, farmers/agricultural households, older adults/caregivers, welfare seekers, small-business owners, and community-service helpers. Include rural/urban and different digital-confidence contexts where feasible. Do not treat any demographic category as a proxy for ability or preference.

Use fictional scenarios and public information, not participants' real case details. Example tasks: locate the official page for a named scheme; explain in their own words which conditions are stated; identify what is unknown; find the official application route; compare the directory summary against a sample current government source after the source audit is complete.

## Method

1. Explain the independent prototype, limits, and data handling; obtain informed consent.
2. Ask the participant to describe their current way of finding public information using a fictional or generic scenario.
3. Observe the same task in CivicLens with think-aloud prompts; do not coach them toward the correct answer.
4. Ask them to identify the source, date, uncertainty, next step, and whether they think CivicLens made an official decision.
5. Ask what was clear, confusing, or untrustworthy and what alternative they would use.
6. Debrief, explain correct routes, and provide a way to withdraw their research notes.

Do not record audio/video unless separately consented and necessary. Prefer anonymous task notes; avoid collecting names, contact details, Aadhaar, bank information, caste/community details, health/disability data, copies of documents, or raw chat transcripts.

## Measures

- Task completion and time, including task failure or referral to a human channel.
- Correct identification of the official source and responsible department.
- Comprehension of supported conditions and unknowns using a short answer key.
- Whether participants mistake a directory lead for confirmed eligibility.
- Language preference and task comprehension, reported separately by the participant.
- Confidence/trust and reasons, captured without attributing sensitive demographics.

Set thresholds after a baseline with current tools; do not select success thresholds after seeing CivicLens results. Include negative results and cases where existing portals or humans work better.

## Stop conditions

Pause a task if the participant starts sharing sensitive personal information, appears distressed, treats a result as a guaranteed benefit decision, or encounters a potentially harmful factual error. Correct misinformation and document the issue without retaining private details.

No interviews or real-user testing have been performed as of this plan.
